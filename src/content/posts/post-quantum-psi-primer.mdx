Private Set Intersection (PSI) is a cryptographic protocol that allows two parties to compute the intersection of their sets without revealing anything beyond the result. It's foundational for privacy-preserving applications: contact list discovery, biometric search, credential verification, and more.

But here's the problem: most PSI schemes are built on discrete logarithm or integer factorization assumptions — assumptions that quantum computers can break in polynomial time (Shor's algorithm). If you're building systems where the identifiers need to remain secure for decades, PSI schemes need post-quantum assumptions.

The notation $\Pi_{\mathsf{PSI}}^{\mathsf{PQ}}$ represents a post-quantum PSI construction — one built on lattices, isogenies, or other quantum-resistant problems. This matters for government databases, healthcare systems, and any long-lived credential storage.
